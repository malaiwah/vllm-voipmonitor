01

Verdict

Is it novel? Mostly no. Is it feasible? Yes — but only a specific, constrained version, and the feasible version is not the proposed one.

The judgment: build the offline half first, prove the value, and only then consider runtime. The rebalancer is the least valuable and most expensive component. The valuable, cheap, genuinely novel part is workload-derived bit allocation applied at startup, persisted and rehydrated.

The go/no-go is not an engineering question. It is: does workload-specialized allocation beat generic allocation at equal bpw? Measurable offline in about a day, with tooling exllamav3 already ships — and it must be answered before a line of runtime code is written.

Already solved elsewhere, by name

The full runtime loop — EMA hotness from live router traces → budget-constrained top-N precision set → asynchronous promote/demote → atomic handoff — is DynaExq (arXiv:2511.15015), a one-for-one match including hysteresis and stable expert handles. Published nine months ago.

The hot-swap mechanism is MorphServe (arXiv:2506.02006): pre-staged variants in pinned host memory, written into pre-reserved GPU slots on async streams, ~6 ms per layer, fully overlapped with decode — a measured rebuttal to "vLLM loads weights once at startup."

Offline "which tensor deserves the bits" is not an open research question: HAWQ→V3 established sensitivity-under-ILP; MC-MoE (arXiv:2410.06270, ICLR 2025) solves per-expert allocation as an LP over (frequency, routing weight, reconstruction error) in about one second.

One premise is false

Allocation does not require BF16 activation measurement on rented B300s. Three cheap or calibration-free signals exist: AlphaQ (weight spectra only), router-norm allocation (arXiv:2604.06515 — "no GPU and negligible computation", and it beats activation-frequency baselines), and KL-lens (forward-only, no backprop, no Hessian).

What is genuinely novel

  1. Runtime precision reallocation under tensor parallelism. Every published system is single-GPU. DynaExq names distributed integration as future work. TP4+DCP4 at EP=1 is unexplored.
  2. Bit allocation for an MTP draft head, with acceptance length as the objective. Nothing in the literature addresses this. The collector work is ahead of published research.
  3. Long-horizon allocation stability. No paper measures it — not even DynaExq, whose premise depends on it and which publishes no rank-correlation across windows. The 7.3M-token layer-78 trace is plausibly the best dataset in existence to answer this.
  4. Online re-encoding. Novel because it is a bad idea, not because nobody thought of it. Drop it.

Why the proposal as written fails

  • All-K4 routed experts need 86.62 GiB/rank against a ~86 GiB budget at util=0.90. Any max-capacity arena is dead on arrival.
  • EXL3 trellis codes are not successively refinable, so every bit-width change is a full re-encode requiring a Hessian the pipeline computes and discards.
  • CUDA-graph pointer baking is enforced systemically throughout vLLM.

(a) Online quantization already exists — the cache does not

A first-class, layer-at-a-time meta-device path ships today: uses_meta_device (base_config.py:21-24), Fp8OnlineLinearMethod, and Mxfp8OnlineMoEMethod (mxfp8.py:224-227). It is even designed to re-run at runtime — the reload path deliberately deletes the _already_called_process_weights_after_loading guard (reload/layerwise.py:219-222).

What is missing is exactly the cache. Nothing under vllm/model_executor/ writes quantized results to disk; every restart re-quantizes from scratch. This is the single best first upstream contribution.

(b) Statistics — nearly free, with two traps

BaseRouter.set_capture_fn (base_router.py:132-134, invoked at :239-241 before EPLB remap) is an ungated per-layer hook, independent of enable_eplb and of expert parallelism.

Trap 1. MoE ops are not CUDA-graph splitting ops (compilation/config.py:1006), so the router runs inside captured graphs. A callback containing .item(), host branching, or list appends silently freezes at capture time and never updates again. It must be a pure tensor op into a persistent buffer — exactly why EPLB's recorder is a single scatter_add_.

Trap 2. The in-tree RoutedExpertsCapturer is unavailable: enable_return_routed_experts is hard-asserted off under DCP>1 (scheduler.py:249-253), which DCP4 trips. The hook is not gated — only the shm return path.

(f) The policy artifact format already exists in your fork

The fork already loads a per-expert bitrate map from a JSON reference in the HF repo, keyed by layer, one integer per expert, k_values restricted to {3,4,5,6} (exl3.py:507-556). That is the policy artifact. One design question eliminated.

Two caveats: K2 is not representable, so the budget-freeing half of the proposal is capped at "don't upgrade"; and the checkpoint hard-fails when checkpoint_tp != runtime tp — storage is topology-baked.

(g) The kernel is your differentiator

Upstream vLLM cannot run a statically mixed-bit MoE layer — issue #41955, mixed INT4/INT8 GPTQ MoE crashes in fused_marlin_moe, because fused kernels assume one bit-width per layer. MxMoE (arXiv:2505.05799) shows autogenerated mixed-precision GroupGEMM is worth 29.4% over uniform at matched accuracy. The b12x two-tier mixed_trellis megakernel already does this. Lead the RFC with it.

03

The hard part

The constraint, stated precisely

A captured CUDA graph bakes device pointers into launch parameters. vLLM's replay-time address check (cuda_graph.py:276-279, 338-347) covers only positional tensor args, never module parameters, and is gated on VLLM_LOGGING_LEVEL == "DEBUG" — off in production. A weight replaced at a new address yields silent wrong output, not an exception.

reload/layerwise.py:240 — "Copy processed values into original tensor storage (preserves cudagraph refs)"

Worse, at the data-structure level: FusedMoE.get_expert_weights() returns weight.view(local_num_experts, -1) with a contiguity assert. Experts are rows of one tensor with uniform stride — per-expert heterogeneous bit-width is not hard in this layout, it is unrepresentable.

The chosen design

Verified directly in the fork's source. api.compile_mixed_trellis(...) (exl3.py:1885-1901) takes tier0_num_experts, tier1_num_experts, tier0_bits, tier1_bits — cardinalities are baked into the compiled launch. But api.run_mixed_trellis(...) (:1955) takes global_to_combined and descriptor_map as runtime device-tensor arguments on every call, produced by build_tiered_maps() purely from two lists of global expert IDs.

Tier membership is runtime data. Tier cardinality is compiled state.

So: hold per-layer cardinalities fixed at startup (|K4| = NL, |K3| = E − NL) and express every rebalance as a membership permutation.

Each stated blocker dissolves. Tier slab shapes are constant forever, so every write is a fixed-shape row copy_ — exactly what EPLB already does safely. No parameter is ever rebound. The tier_signature is invariant, so the runtime cache hits forever and the RuntimeError at :1862 is never reached. Fragmentation is zero because no allocation occurs on the swap path. And online re-encoding is eliminated — page pre-built artifacts, as every published system does.

At the current 3.42 bpw operating point: N = 108 experts/layer at K4, 148 at K3, total 74.07 GiB/rank — leaving ~12 GiB for dense/attention, the MTP draft, the ~1 GiB Trellis arena, activations, NCCL, graphs and KV. A 108-slot swappable population per layer is ample.

Swap cost. One expert exchanged per layer moves 7.875 MiB/rank; a full-model pass over 77 layers moves ~606 MiB/rank — 11–24 ms of H2D on PCIe, fully overlappable on a side stream. Reserved staging: 9 MiB/rank best case, allocated inside profile_run under an is_profile flag so the KV budget shrinks honestly — EPLB's exact trick. Offline storage: full K3 + K4 artifacts ≈ 607 GB on NVMe. Trivial, and it buys away Viterbi cost, Hessian persistence, and the entire online-quantization subsystem.

The one open question, and why it doesn't block

Whether b12x exposes a per-expert row write into a prepared tier object is not visible from the authorized repos. Best case: swap is two row copies plus map rebuild — sub-millisecond, 9 MiB reserved. Fallback: rebuild one layer's two tier slabs, ~0.96 GiB/rank (~1.92 GiB double-buffered), one layer per engine step — EPLB's exact cadence. Answering it chooses between 9 MiB and 2 GiB of reserved memory; it does not change whether the design works.

Failure modes

  • Torn update between trellis rows and suh/svh/rotations corrupts silently. Do the whole metadata update inside the quiesce window, or double-buffer and flip an index. vLLM ships the primitive: pause_scheduler(mode="keep").
  • Strict permutation — every upgrade costs a downgrade. Also a feature: the budget becomes a structural invariant, not a checked constraint.
  • Two-tier ceiling — len(tiers) != 2 raises (:1591); K2 unavailable.
  • Cross-rank divergence is fatal. A per-logical-expert policy applied uniformly gives bit-identity for free.
  • FusedMoEQuantConfig staleness — memoized with direct tensor references; any swap must null it.
04

The policy problem

Do not use raw hotness

Two independent papers found it is the weakest available signal — the router-norm method explicitly beats activation-frequency baselines, and MoPEQ uses Hessian trace "instead of relying on activation frequency." vLLM's EPLB counter records literally nothing else: scatter_add_ of ones_like.

Three signal terms, all cheap, all topology-neutral

  1. Static error curve εe(b) — pure weight-space, computable locally with no rented hardware. Free extra priors: AlphaQ's spectral α, router-vector L2 norm.
  2. Workload term — routing mass (Σ gate values), not counts, plus frequency, over the rolling window. HOBBIT's LHU ("how often was this expert needed at high precision") is a strictly better promotion signal than raw usage.
  3. Verification term — measured end-to-end KL against a held-out probe, by actually applying a candidate swap. This is the one thing a live server can do that an offline quantizer cannot.

The objective must be KL, never perplexity. TASA shows PPL-based layer sensitivity has Kendall τ ≈ 0 with reasoning-task sensitivity, and that PPL-sensitive layers cluster at embeddings and the output head while reasoning-critical layers sit mid-network — PPL-guided allocation systematically under-protects reasoning. exllamav3's measure_model.py already uses KL.

Under fixed cardinality, the LP degenerates to a sort

With two tiers and NL fixed, rank experts by marginal utility Δe = (εe,3 − εe,4) · φeα · weβ and take the top NL. That is argsort. Reserve the full LP for choosing per-layer NL once at startup from a global solve; the online loop only permutes membership within a layer.

Two corrections the naive design gets wrong

  • EvoPress (ICML 2025) shows per-layer error is not monotone or additive — "pruning a model further may even significantly recover performance." Every greedy allocator assumes the separability this refutes. Consequence: cap at 1–4 swaps per layer per interval, validate against the held-out KL probe, and roll back.
  • TASA shows calibrating purely on in-domain data actively degrades quality — pure task data makes the layer-wise Hessian eigenspectrum degenerate. Optimal mixing is model-specific (50% general for LLaMA-3, 75% for Qwen2.5). So "specialize" means reweight, not replace: derive ε from a blended set, use live traffic only for the φ/w weighting.

Topology neutrality — two artifacts, deliberately separated

  • Policy artifact (neutral). logical expert id → bits, keyed on checkpoint identity, base quant config, schema version, bit menu. Explicitly excludes rank, world_size, tp_size, device count, device name.
  • Derived slab cache (topology-keyed, regenerable). Per-rank materialization.

Both existing precedents violate this: startup_plan.compute_plan_fingerprint includes rank and world_size, VllmConfig.compute_hash() folds in parallel_config, and the checkpoint carries rank{r} in tensor names. The rank-slicing is a storage choice — the trellis slices cleanly at 16-column granularity because every 16×16 tile is a self-contained tail-biting codeword.

Convenient accident: because EP is off, every TP rank routes the same tokens, so per-expert counts are logical, not physical — already topology-neutral. Under EP they would not be.

Feedback loops — real, and completely unstudied

The loop precision change → output change → router logit shift → top-8 flip → different hotness → different decision is real. Four groups establish that quantization shifts routing (EAQuant, MoEQuant, ExpertQuant, EAC-MoE); ExpertQuant finds errors dominated by near-neighbor rank flips around top-k — and with top-8 of 256, the 8th/9th margin is thin. No published work connects this to a runtime loop. Mitigations in cost order: keep the router in BF16 (this stack already does); rollback guard on top-8 Jaccard overlap vs BF16 ground truth (the collector captures exactly this); hysteresis; dwell time; a hard cap on swaps per interval.

Cold start: never start from uniform. Ship a generic allocation as the prior and rehydrate the previous session's policy — which, given fixed cardinality, is literally "load the JSON that already exists."

Does EXL3 not being upstream block this?

No — if the design is format-agnostic. Yes — if you bundle EXL3. Upstream has declined EXL3 four times (#19896 closed not-planned; #11416 QTIP closed; #3203, #296, #2645 closed unimplemented), and #39583/#30136 show active de-scoping of exotic formats to out-of-tree plugins.

Define the interface over a capability, not a format:

supports_variable_bitwidth -> bool
available_bitwidths(tensor_name) -> tuple[int, ...]
materialize(tensor_name, bits)  -> (opaque_bytes, length)

vLLM core owns statistics, policy, budget, scheduling and the swap transaction. EXL3 ships out-of-tree via register_quantization_config; FP8, INT4, MXFP4 and LUT-B can all implement the same interface. This is the correct engineering boundary regardless of upstreaming.

The strongest hook into upstream's current interests is PR #50168 — a core maintainer prototyping NVIDIA Rubin LUT-B at 3.125–3.516 bpw, with a calibration-free accuracy gap of 52.08% → 75.51% GSM8K depending on variant. That gap is the "which tensors deserve the bits" problem, in the same bpw band, from a maintainer. Natural reviewer, right venue.

What the research could not establish

  • b12x / SparkInfer internals — not in the authorized repos. The decisive question was resolved from the vLLM-side caller, but repack-vs-alias, per-expert row write, and >2 tiers remain unread.
  • EXL3 encode cost on SM120 — K-independence of Viterbi compute is proven from the kernel source; real K3-vs-K4 timings could still differ by tens of percent.
  • GLM-5.2 routing behaviour — every published expert-activation measurement is on Mixtral, Qwen-MoE, DeepSeek-MoE, OLMoE. Nothing measures GLM-5.2. Your own trace is better evidence than any published paper — which is exactly why Phase 0 runs first.
  • The right objective for an MTP draft head — acceptance length vs KL vs PPL — is genuinely ahead of the literature. No external validation available.
  • DynaExq has no code release found; its EMA α and update period are undisclosed.
  • Maintainer sentiment is unestablished. Five minutes of reading issue comments closes this.

The bottom line

Two published results (VBQ, AWQ) and one measurement (MoE-Infinity's "reuse counts even out over time") all predict that K3 fires before K1 — that the allocation converges and stops moving, in which case a one-time warmup specialization captures the value and the continuous machinery is wasted. Phase 1 is designed to be exactly that product, and Phase 0a will tell you within a day of pandas work.

Build Phase 0. Then decide.
